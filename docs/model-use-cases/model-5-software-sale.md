# Model 5: Software Sale with Ongoing Support — Use Cases

> **Purpose**: When to use each variant of the Software Sale model
> **Variants**: 8 (5A–5H)
> **Core Concept**: Developer sells completed software with full IP transfer; optional ongoing support

---

## Overview

Model 5 is best when **Developer wants a clean, complete exit** from ownership. Buyer purchases the software outright, capitalises it as an asset, and may engage Developer for optional maintenance. Final transaction — no ongoing ownership entanglement.

**Choose Model 5 over other models when:**
- Developer wants to fully exit (no retained IP)
- Buyer wants complete ownership and control
- One-time transaction preferred over ongoing royalties
- Clean asset transfer for accounting purposes

---

## Variant 5A: Outright Sale (Upfront)

### Description
Full payment at closing. Complete ownership transfer. Simplest possible transaction.

### Best Scenario
**Developer wants complete exit** with no ongoing entanglement, and Buyer has cash available and internal support capability.

### Real-World Example
> A boutique software firm sells their HR management platform to a larger competitor. Price: R15M paid at closing. Full IP, source code, documentation, and customer contracts transfer. Developer derecognises asset entirely. Buyer adds to their product portfolio. Clean break — no installments, no earnouts, no ongoing fees.

### Why 5A Over Other Variants
- Simplest transaction structure
- Complete certainty (no deferred elements)
- Developer fully exits immediately
- Clean accounting for both parties

### Transfer Pricing Consideration
Sale price must be arm's length. Document comparable transactions or independent valuation.

---

## Variant 5B: Instalment Sale

### Description
Payment spread over time (e.g., 3–5 years). Developer effectively finances the acquisition.

### Best Scenario
**Buyer's cash flow stretched** — cannot pay lump sum but can pay over time. Developer willing to finance.

### Real-World Example
> A mid-size company purchases ERP software for R6M but can't afford full upfront payment. Structure: R1.5M at closing + R1.5M annually for 3 years (implicit 8% interest). Buyer manages cash flow. Developer receives R7.35M total (including interest). Asset transfers at closing; payments continue.

### Why 5B Over Other Variants
- Enables sales to cash-constrained Buyers
- Developer earns interest income on financing
- Buyer's total cost may exceed 5A but cash flow manageable
- Common for material acquisitions

### Transfer Pricing Consideration
Document that implicit interest rate is arm's length. Present value should approximate fair market value.

---

## Variant 5C: Deferred Payment

### Description
Single lump sum payment deferred to future date. Buyer gets time to monetise before paying.

### Best Scenario
**Buyer needs time to generate returns** — acquires software, deploys it, generates revenue, then pays from proceeds.

### Real-World Example
> A startup acquires analytics software to enhance their platform. Contract: Software transfers immediately, R4M payment due in 18 months. Startup uses the 18 months to integrate software, launch enhanced product, and generate revenue to fund the payment. Deferred payment (including time value) = R4.5M.

### Why 5C Over Other Variants
- Buyer's cash outflow timed with revenue generation
- Lower immediate capital requirement than 5A
- Simpler than instalment structure (one future payment)
- Developer compensated for time value

### Transfer Pricing Consideration
Deferred price should reflect time value adjustment. Document discount rate used.

---

## Variant 5D: Sale with SLA-Based Maintenance

### Description
Software sale plus maintenance contract with fees tied to service level performance.

### Best Scenario
**Ongoing performance matters** — Buyer needs software to meet uptime/response SLAs after purchase.

### Real-World Example
> A logistics company purchases route optimisation software for R3M. Maintenance contract: R300k/year base + R100k bonus if 99.9% uptime maintained + R50k penalty if response time SLA missed. Developer incentivised to provide excellent support. Buyer gets performance guarantees.

### Why 5D Over Other Variants
- Maintenance quality aligned with Buyer needs
- Developer incentivised for excellence
- Buyer gets SLA guarantees
- Common for business-critical systems

### Transfer Pricing Consideration
Sale price and maintenance fees separately benchmarked. SLA adjustments documented.

---

## Variant 5E: Source Code Sale

### Description
Sale includes full source code access. Buyer gains complete independence for self-maintenance.

### Best Scenario
**Buyer wants full control and independence** — ability to maintain, modify, and extend without Developer involvement.

### Real-World Example
> A manufacturing company purchases production scheduling software including full source code for R5M (vs R3.5M binary-only). They hire their own developers to maintain and customise. Complete independence from original Developer. No vendor lock-in. Can modify freely.

### Why 5E Over Other Variants
- Buyer's complete independence
- No future Developer dependency
- Buyer can modify/extend freely
- Higher price reflects independence value

### Transfer Pricing Consideration
Source code premium (typically 20–50%) should be benchmarked. Document comparable source code vs binary transactions.

---

## Variant 5F: Sale with Extended Warranty

### Description
Extended defect warranty period (24–36 months). Developer liable for defects found post-sale.

### Best Scenario
**Quality assurance critical** — Buyer needs protection against hidden defects in complex software.

### Real-World Example
> A financial services firm purchases trading software. Given complexity and regulatory requirements, contract includes 36-month warranty. If critical defects discovered, Developer must remediate at no cost. Warranty premium: R500k added to R8M base price. Buyer protected against latent issues.

### Why 5F Over Other Variants
- Extended protection for Buyer
- Developer stands behind quality
- Reduces Buyer's post-sale risk
- Appropriate for complex/regulated systems

### Transfer Pricing Consideration
Warranty premium should reflect arm's length cost of risk assumed. Document comparable warranty arrangements.

---

## Variant 5G: Sale with Earnout

### Description
Base purchase price plus performance-contingent earnout. Bridges valuation gap between parties.

### Best Scenario
**Valuation disagreement** — Developer believes software worth more than Buyer willing to pay upfront. Earnout bridges gap.

### Real-World Example
> Developer wants R12M for their CRM platform. Buyer values it at R8M. Solution: R8M at closing + earnout of 10% of annual revenue generated by platform for 3 years. If platform generates R15M/year → R1.5M/year earnout → R4.5M total earnout. Developer gets up to R12.5M if software performs. Buyer pays full price only if value delivered.

### Why 5G Over Other Variants
- Bridges valuation gap
- Developer gets upside if software outperforms
- Buyer's risk reduced (pays premium only if earned)
- Enables deals that otherwise wouldn't happen

### Transfer Pricing Consideration
Earnout terms should be arm's length. Document probability-weighted expected value at sale.

---

## Variant 5H: Sale with Transition Support

### Description
Sale includes time-limited handover period with training, documentation, and initial support.

### Best Scenario
**Complex software requiring knowledge transfer** — Buyer's team needs to learn the system.

### Real-World Example
> A hospital acquires patient management software. Contract: R6M purchase price + R400k for 6-month transition support. Transition includes: 2 weeks onsite training, full documentation handover, dedicated support contact for 6 months, bug fixes during transition. After 6 months, Buyer's team is self-sufficient.

### Why 5H Over Other Variants
- Smooth handover reduces implementation risk
- Buyer's team trained and capable
- Reduces post-sale support burden
- Standard practice for complex acquisitions

### Transfer Pricing Consideration
Transition support fee should reflect arm's length cost of services. Document scope and duration.

---

## Variant Selection Guide

| Scenario | Best Variant |
|----------|--------------|
| Simple transaction, clean exit | **5A** — outright sale |
| Buyer cash-constrained, spread payments | **5B** — instalment sale |
| Buyer needs time to monetise | **5C** — deferred payment |
| Ongoing performance SLAs matter | **5D** — SLA-based maintenance |
| Buyer wants complete independence | **5E** — source code sale |
| Quality assurance critical | **5F** — extended warranty |
| Valuation disagreement | **5G** — earnout |
| Complex software, knowledge transfer needed | **5H** — transition support |

---

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Using 5A when Buyer can't afford lump sum** — deal falls through (use 5B/5C)
2. **Skipping 5H transition for complex software** — Buyer struggles post-sale
3. **Underpricing 5E source code premium** — leaving value on table
4. **Earnout metrics in 5G not clearly defined** — disputes over calculation
